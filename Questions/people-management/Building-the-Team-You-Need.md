# Building the Team You Need

> Tell me about a hire — or a role you deliberately decided not to fill — that shaped your team's ability to execute. Walk me through your reasoning.

> **Follow-up** Tell me about a time you restructured a team — split it, merged it, or changed reporting lines. What business need drove that, and what was the outcome?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## People Management Competency

[Hiring & Team Composition](../../docs/people-management-competency.md#hiring--team-composition) (also relevant to [Organization Design](../../docs/people-management-competency.md#organization-design))

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles that manage other PMs — see [people-management-competency.md](../../docs/people-management-competency.md). Team composition and structure decisions compound over quarters and years; a leader who hires reactively or defaults to a familiar org shape without real reasoning will eventually build a team that can't execute the actual strategy. This question tests whether a candidate reasons deliberately about who they hire and how the team is shaped, including the discipline to decide against filling a role.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What made this hire (or non-hire) different from the obvious choice?
- What would have happened if you'd made the more conventional decision instead?
- How did the team actually respond to the restructure — any real friction?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific hiring or team-composition decision with real reasoning tied to what the team actually needed, not a generic "we needed more capacity."
2. If describing a non-hire, explains what they did instead to cover the gap.
3. In the follow-up, ties a real structural change to a specific business need, not an org-chart preference.
4. Gives an honest account of the outcome, including any real friction or cost the decision created.

## Warning Signs

1. No real reasoning behind the hiring decision — just "we needed someone."
2. The restructure in the follow-up is described with no real business driver.
3. No acknowledgment of any friction or downside from the decision — implausibly smooth outcome.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific hiring/composition decision with real reasoning, a genuine business-driven restructure, and an honest account of friction or cost.
- **3:** A real decision is described but the reasoning or the outcome account is thin.
- **1:** No real reasoning behind either decision described.
