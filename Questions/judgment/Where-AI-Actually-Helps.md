# Where AI Actually Helps

> Tell me about a time you decided *not* to add AI to a product or feature, even though someone wanted you to. What made you say no?

> **Follow-up** Now tell me about a time you did add it. What made that case genuinely different?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Opportunity Discovery](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-opportunity-discovery)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). For those roles, the pressure to add AI to a feature regardless of whether it actually helps is real and constant. This question tests whether a candidate has a genuine criterion for where AI adds value versus where it's decoration, and whether they'll hold that line under pressure.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Who wanted the AI added, and what did you say to them?
- What would have had to be true for AI to actually be the right call there?
- In the case where you did add it, what evidence told you it was actually the right call, not just a plausible one?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific real request to add AI and a specific reason it was declined — tied to customer outcome, workflow fit, or economics, not just "it felt unnecessary."
2. Describes how they held that position under real pressure from the requester.
3. In the follow-up, the "yes" case has a genuinely different, articulable reason — not the same justification applied inconsistently.
4. Shows a real criterion for "AI adds value here" that could be applied to a *different* feature, not just post-hoc rationalization of one decision.

## Warning Signs

1. No real declined request — a hypothetical or trivial example.
2. The "no" and "yes" cases don't actually differ in any principled way.
3. Reasoning for either decision is vague ("it just made sense") with no criterion behind it.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific declined request with clear reasoning held under pressure, and a genuinely distinct, well-reasoned "yes" case in the follow-up.
- **3:** Real examples exist but the reasoning criterion is thin or inconsistently applied.
- **1:** No real example, or no principled distinction between the "no" and "yes" cases.
