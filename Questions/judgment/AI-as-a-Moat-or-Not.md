# AI as a Moat, or Not

> Tell me about a case where you had to decide whether an AI capability was a real, defensible advantage for your product — or something any competitor could copy in a quarter. How did you decide, and what did you do with that answer?

> **Follow-up** What would actually make an AI capability hard to copy — proprietary data, workflow integration, distribution, something else?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Strategy & Competitive Advantage](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-strategy--competitive-advantage)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). It's easy to treat an AI feature as a strategic advantage simply because it's new; it's harder to reason honestly about whether it's actually defensible. This question tests whether a candidate can make that distinction and act differently depending on the answer.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What made you conclude it was, or wasn't, defensible?
- What did you do differently once you had that answer — invest more, invest less, focus on something else entirely?
- Has a competitor actually copied it since? What happened?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific real AI capability and a specific reasoned judgment about its defensibility — not a generic "AI is a differentiator" claim.
2. Names the actual source of defensibility considered — proprietary data, workflow lock-in, distribution advantage, or the honest conclusion that there wasn't one.
3. Describes a real strategic action that followed from the judgment — doubling down, redirecting investment, or accepting the feature as table stakes rather than a moat.
4. In the follow-up, gives specific reasoning about what generally makes AI capabilities hard to copy, not a generic answer.

## Warning Signs

1. Assumes any AI capability is automatically a competitive advantage with no real analysis.
2. No real strategic action followed from the judgment — the analysis was academic.
3. Follow-up answer is generic rather than reasoned from the specific case.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific capability, genuine defensibility reasoning naming a real source (or honestly concluding there wasn't one), and a real strategic action that followed.
- **3:** A real case exists but the reasoning or the resulting action is thin.
- **1:** Assumes AI is inherently defensible with no real analysis, or no action followed from the judgment.
