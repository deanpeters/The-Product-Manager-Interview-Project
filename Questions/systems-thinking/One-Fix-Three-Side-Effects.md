# One Fix, Three Side Effects

> You improve one part of a system dramatically: faster approval, lower friction, more automation, cheaper supply, or higher conversion. What might get worse somewhere else?

> **Follow-up** Which side effect would you watch first, and what signal would tell you the fix is creating harm?

## Type

Systems Thinking Exercise

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning
- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## Why This Matters

Product changes often create local wins and system-level costs. This exercise tests whether a candidate can reason about second-order effects, local optimization, and unintended consequences before declaring a fix successful.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Who benefits immediately from the improvement, and who might absorb the cost later?
- What metric could improve while the overall system gets worse?
- What new behavior might this create for users, teams, partners, or bad actors?
- What guardrail would you put beside the primary success metric?
- How would you decide whether the trade-off is acceptable?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names multiple plausible side effects across different parts of the system, not just a single obvious downside.
2. Distinguishes local optimization from system health.
3. Identifies a guardrail or counter-metric that could reveal hidden harm.
4. Connects side effects to actor incentives and behavior changes.
5. Describes how they would make the trade-off explicit before scaling the fix.

## Warning Signs

1. Assumes a local improvement is automatically a system improvement.
2. Cannot identify who might be worse off after the fix.
3. Treats guardrail metrics as bureaucracy rather than learning.
4. Hand-waves harm as "edge cases" without reasoning about likelihood or severity.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Identifies credible second-order effects, explains who carries the cost, chooses useful guardrails, and makes the trade-off explicit before scaling.
- **3:** Recognizes that side effects are possible, but the examples or monitoring plan are generic.
- **1:** Declares the fix successful based only on the improved local metric.

## Role Or Level Notes

This is especially useful for AI, growth, marketplace, platform, internal tools, and operations-heavy roles. For senior candidates, expect them to include organizational and ethical side effects, not only user-experience side effects.
