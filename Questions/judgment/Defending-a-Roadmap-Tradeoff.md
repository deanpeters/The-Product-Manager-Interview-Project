# Defending a Roadmap Trade-off

> Walk me through a roadmap where you deliberately left something important off. What did you cut, and how did you defend that to the people who wanted it?

> **Follow-up** What dependency or capacity constraint almost got missed until someone else caught it?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Delivery, Execution, And Ownership

## Why This Matters

A roadmap is a sequence of things not being done as much as it's a sequence of things being done. This question tests whether a candidate can name a real exclusion, defend it with evidence rather than authority, and hold that line when someone with more positional power wants it back on the roadmap. Use the [evidence ladder](../../docs/evidence-and-pressure-standard.md) when probing what the "defense" was actually built on.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What evidence told you the thing you cut mattered less than what you kept — an estimate, a report from someone else, or something you measured yourself?
- Who pushed back hardest, and what did you say to them?
- Did the exclusion turn out to be right? How do you know?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific, real exclusion — not a hypothetical or a trivially unimportant item.
2. Explains the reasoning for the cut in terms an interviewer could evaluate — capacity, evidence of lower impact, sequencing dependency — not just "it felt lower priority."
3. Describes an actual pushback conversation and how they held or adjusted their position based on what the other person brought, not just repeated their original point.
4. Names the dependency or capacity constraint in the follow-up specifically, and how it was caught.

## Warning Signs

1. The "exclusion" is vague or clearly wasn't a real contested trade-off.
2. Reasoning for the cut is asserted with no evidence behind it, or evidence pitched at a higher confidence than it earns (see the evidence ladder).
3. No real account of defending the decision under pushback — just "everyone agreed."

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific exclusion, evidence-grounded reasoning with the right confidence level, a real defended pushback conversation, and a named dependency catch in the follow-up.
- **3:** Real exclusion and reasoning exist, but the pushback account or the dependency follow-up is thin.
- **1:** No real trade-off, or reasoning is asserted with no evidence.

## Role Or Level Notes

For Director/VP-level candidates, raise pressure per [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](../../docs/evidence-and-pressure-standard.md): press on whether the "capacity constraint" was actually measured or just assumed, and ask what they'd do differently if the same trade-off came up again with twice the stakeholders in the room.
