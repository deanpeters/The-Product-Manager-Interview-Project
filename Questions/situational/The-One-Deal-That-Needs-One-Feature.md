# The One Deal That Needs One Feature

> A salesperson comes to you insisting, "If we just build feature X, we'll close this deal." Walk me through how you'd actually handle that request.

> **Follow-up** How do you tell the difference between a real market signal and a one-off request dressed up as one?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Revenue-motivated, one-off feature requests are one of the most common ways a product gets cluttered with things almost nobody else uses. This question tests whether a candidate can work with a sales-driven request constructively — neither building on faith nor dismissing it outright — by actually investigating whether it represents a real, broader problem.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would you actually ask the salesperson, and what would you go check yourself?
- What would make you say yes to this request as-is?
- What would make you say no, even if it costs that specific deal?

## Strong Evidence

1. Works alongside the salesperson rather than simply refusing or simply queuing the feature.
2. Asks what underlying problem the requested feature is meant to solve, not just accepting the solution as stated.
3. Checks whether this reflects a broader pattern — other customers, competitive research — rather than treating one anecdote as sufficient evidence.
4. Shares the real complexity or cost involved rather than letting an unrealistic "we can make that happen" go unchallenged.

## Warning Signs

1. Builds the feature purely on the salesperson's assertion, with no validation.
2. Refuses the request without engaging or investigating further.
3. No real method for distinguishing a broad signal from a one-off ask.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Genuine collaboration with the salesperson, a real investigation into the underlying problem and its breadth, and honest cost/complexity communication.
- **3:** Some investigation happens but is thin, or the candidate leans toward simply complying or simply refusing.
- **1:** No real investigation — the request is either built on faith or dismissed outright.
