# Sizing a Market You Can Defend

> Size the market for [a product relevant to the role] — bottom-up, not top-down. Walk me through your segments, your math, and which input you're least confident in.

> **Follow-up** For your key inputs, which are facts, which are inferences, and which are assumptions? Give me a range, not just a point estimate.

## Type

Method

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Unlike a generic "how many pianos are in this building" estimation puzzle — see [`Questions/legacy/Number-of-Pianos.md`](../legacy/Number-of-Pianos.md), which tests general Fermi-estimation reasoning rather than real market-sizing method — this card tests whether a candidate can actually build a defensible market model: bottom-up per segment, not a top-down percentage of a big number, with sourced inputs and honest uncertainty. This is the "future rubric" earlier flagged in [`docs/question-bank-triage.md`](../../docs/question-bank-triage.md) as the condition for bringing a market-sizing exercise back into active use.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Walk me through the actual arithmetic for one segment — units, price, frequency.
- What's your source or benchmark for that number, even if it's a rough one?
- Which single input, if wrong, would break the whole model?
- Why that range and not a tighter or wider one?

## Strong Evidence

1. Builds the estimate bottom-up per segment (a specific buyer x geography x need combination), not top-down as a percentage of a large aggregate number.
2. Shows real arithmetic — units, price, frequency — not just a final number asserted with confidence.
3. Distinguishes which inputs are facts (directly known), inferences (reasoned from related facts), and assumptions (genuinely unverified), using something like the [evidence ladder](../../docs/evidence-and-pressure-standard.md) even if not by that name.
4. Gives a range with a stated driver of the spread, not a single falsely precise number.
5. Proactively identifies their own weakest input rather than being caught out by the follow-up.

## Warning Signs

1. Top-down reasoning ("if we get just 1% of a $50B market...") with no bottom-up segment math underneath.
2. A single confident number with no acknowledgment of uncertainty.
3. Cannot distinguish a fact from an assumption when asked directly.
4. Segments a market as a single homogeneous block rather than distinct buyer/need combinations.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Real bottom-up segment math, sourced or clearly-labeled inputs, an honest fact/inference/assumption breakdown, a real range, and a self-identified weak point.
- **3:** Attempts bottom-up reasoning but leans on unsourced guesses, or the range/weak-input identification only appears after prompting.
- **1:** Pure top-down guessing, or a single unhedged number presented as fact.

## Role Or Level Notes

For a role where market sizing is a core skill (strategy, new-market-entry, or business-development-adjacent PM roles), this can replace or supplement a generic prioritization question in Round 2 of the [core interview loop](../../docs/core-interview-loop.md).
