# Assumptions Worth Testing First

> You have a promising product idea. What assumptions would worry you most, and how would you decide which one to test first?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Customer Understanding And Discovery

## Why This Matters

Every product idea rests on a stack of assumptions — that customers have the problem, that they'd choose this solution, that it's technically feasible, that it's viable for the business. Good product judgment means identifying which assumption is riskiest and testing that one cheaply before committing real investment.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Why is that assumption the one that worries you most, versus the others?
- How would you actually test it — what's the cheapest way to get real evidence?
- What would "wrong" look like, and what would you do if the test confirmed your worry?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names several distinct assumptions (customer problem, solution fit, feasibility, viability) rather than treating the idea as one big untested block.
2. Prioritizes based on risk and cost of being wrong — which assumption, if false, would be most damaging or expensive to discover late.
3. Proposes a genuinely cheap, fast way to test the assumption, not a full build.
4. Has a real answer for what happens if the test disproves the assumption — not just "we'd figure it out."

## Warning Signs

1. Treats the idea as a single monolithic bet with no assumption decomposition.
2. Picks the easiest assumption to test rather than the riskiest one to be wrong about.
3. Proposed test is effectively a full build in disguise.
4. No plan for what happens if the assumption turns out to be false.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Clear decomposition of assumptions, sound risk-based prioritization, a genuinely lightweight test, and a real plan for a disconfirming result.
- **3:** Identifies some assumptions and a plausible test, but the risk-based reasoning for what to test first is thin.
- **1:** No real decomposition of the idea into testable assumptions.
