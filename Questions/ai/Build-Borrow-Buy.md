# Build / Borrow / Buy

> Tell me about a Build / Borrow / Buy decision you made for an AI capability: build it yourselves, borrow it through a third-party cloud service, or buy/acquire the capability. What tipped the decision?

> **Follow-up** What did you get wrong about the cost, latency, control, or lock-in tradeoffs going in?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Economics & Technical Tradeoffs](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-economics--technical-tradeoffs)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). Build / Borrow / Buy is a sourcing decision, not a pause-or-pursue decision: do we create the capability ourselves, rely on a third-party cloud service, or buy/acquire the capability outright? This question tests whether a candidate can reason through cost, latency, control, lock-in, differentiation, and speed without pretending one answer is universally mature.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specific factors tipped it toward build, borrow, or buy?
- What would have made a different choice the right one?
- Was the miss on cost, latency, quality, control, lock-in, or something else entirely?
- What would make "borrow" a smart bridge versus a dependency trap?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific real decision with the actual factors weighed — cost, latency, quality, control, lock-in, differentiation, time to market — not just "we decided to build it."
2. Distinguishes the three paths clearly: build internally, borrow through a third-party cloud/service provider, or buy/acquire the capability.
3. Explains what would have made a different choice correct, showing the decision was a genuine trade-off, not an inevitability.
4. Honestly names what was wrong about the initial estimate or assumption, with real numbers or a real magnitude if possible.
5. In the follow-up, gives a specific, honest account of the actual-versus-estimated gap.

## Warning Signs

1. No real trade-off reasoning — the choice is presented as obviously correct with no serious alternative considered.
2. Treats borrowing a service as the same as buying a capability, or treats buying as the same as partnering.
3. No honest account of what was wrong about the initial estimate.
4. Follow-up reveals no real tracking of actual cost, latency, quality, or dependency risk against the original assumption.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific real decision with genuine Build / Borrow / Buy trade-off reasoning, a clear distinction between the three paths, an honestly named estimate miss, and a real actual-vs-estimated comparison in the follow-up.
- **3:** A real decision exists but the path distinction, estimate-miss honesty, or trade-off reasoning is thin.
- **1:** No real trade-off considered, or no distinction between building, borrowing, and buying.
