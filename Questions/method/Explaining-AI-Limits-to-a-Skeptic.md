# Explaining AI Limits to a Skeptic

> Explain to me, as if I were a smart but non-technical stakeholder, why an AI feature might confidently give a wrong answer. Then tell me about one real product decision that changed because of that limitation.

> **Follow-up** What's a common AI capability people tend to overestimate, and one they tend to underestimate?

## Type

Method

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Fundamentals](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-fundamentals)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md) for why. For those roles, a PM who can't explain a core AI limitation in plain language will struggle to set realistic expectations with stakeholders, scope a feature responsibly, or know when a strange output is a bug versus expected model behavior.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Can you make that even simpler — how would you say it to someone outside the industry entirely?
- What's the real product decision this changed, specifically?
- Where's the line between "the model got it wrong" and "we asked it something it was never going to get right"?

## Strong Evidence

1. Explains a real limitation (e.g., hallucination, lack of grounding, probabilistic rather than deterministic output) in genuinely plain language, not jargon repackaged.
2. Connects it to one specific, real product decision — a scope cut, a UI change to signal uncertainty, a guardrail added — not a hypothetical.
3. Names both an overestimated and an underestimated capability with real reasoning, showing calibrated judgment rather than either AI-skepticism or AI-hype.

## Warning Signs

1. Explanation relies on jargon without a plain-language translation.
2. No real product decision connected to the limitation — it stays abstract.
3. Only names overestimation or only underestimation, suggesting a one-sided view of AI capability.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Clear plain-language explanation, a specific real decision it changed, and a genuinely calibrated view of both over- and under-estimated capabilities.
- **3:** Explanation is reasonable but leans on jargon, or the connected decision is vague.
- **1:** Cannot explain the limitation in accessible terms, or has no real connected decision.
