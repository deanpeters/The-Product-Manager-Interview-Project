# Frankensoft or AI-First

> A mature product has had AI features bolted onto its existing workflows one at a time over the last couple of years — a suggestion here, a summarization there — each one shipped as its own project. How would you tell whether that incremental approach is actually the right move, or whether the underlying workflow needs to be redesigned around what AI now makes possible?

> **Follow-up** What evidence would tell you it's time to stop bolting on and start over?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Strategy & Competitive Advantage](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-strategy--competitive-advantage)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). "AI as a Moat, or Not" tests whether a candidate can judge if an AI capability is genuinely defensible. This card tests a prior, more architectural question that has to be answered before defensibility is even relevant: is the product's AI strategy a series of bolt-on features grafted onto workflows designed for a pre-AI world, or a deliberate redesign of the workflow around what's now possible? A candidate who defaults to "just keep adding features" without ever asking this question is optimizing locally while missing a much larger strategic call — and a candidate who wants to rebuild everything around AI without a real trigger for doing so is showing the opposite failure mode.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would incremental bolt-on AI start costing the product over time, if anything?
- What would a workflow redesigned around AI actually look like differently, concretely — not just "more AI"?
- How would you sequence a redesign like that without breaking what's currently working?
- How do you avoid rebuilding just because it's the more exciting option?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific, concrete cost of the bolt-on approach (inconsistent user mental model, redundant AI calls, a workflow that fights the AI features layered onto it) rather than a vague sense that bolt-on is inferior.
2. Describes what a genuine redesign would look like in specific terms, not just "AI-first" as a slogan.
3. Has a real trigger or threshold for when redesign becomes worth the cost and risk — not a permanent bias toward either incrementalism or rebuilding.
4. Shows awareness of the sequencing/migration risk in a redesign and how they'd manage it.

## Warning Signs

1. Can't articulate any real cost to the bolt-on approach — treats it as obviously fine or obviously wrong with no reasoning.
2. "Redesign around AI" stays abstract — no concrete description of what would actually be different.
3. No real trigger condition — the answer is driven by preference for one approach over the other rather than evidence.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Names a specific real cost of the incremental path, describes a concrete redesigned alternative, gives a real evidence-based trigger for switching, and addresses migration risk.
- **3:** Reasonable instinct toward one approach or the other but thin on specifics or triggers.
- **1:** No real reasoning distinguishing the two paths — defaults to whichever sounds more exciting or more comfortable with no evidence behind it.
