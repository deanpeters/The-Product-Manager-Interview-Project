# The Precedent That Transfers

> Tell me about a product problem that felt new. Where would you look for analogous systems, and how would you decide what transfers versus what does not?

> **Follow-up** Give me one precedent that looks attractive at first but would be dangerous to copy too literally. What breaks in the comparison?

## Type

Systems Thinking Exercise

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning

## Why This Matters

Good product judgment often comes from recognizing useful patterns without pretending two systems are identical. This exercise tests whether a candidate can use precedents as structured evidence: find an analogy, decompose it, extract what transfers, and name what must be tested before borrowing the idea.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would make one system meaningfully analogous to another?
- What parts of the precedent are structural, and what parts are just surface similarity?
- What conditions would have to be true for the precedent to transfer?
- What would you test before recommending the borrowed approach?
- Who might be harmed or ignored if you copied the precedent too directly?

## Strong Evidence

1. Searches beyond direct competitors or obvious category twins, while still explaining why the analogy is relevant.
2. Decomposes the precedent into transferable parts: actors, incentives, constraints, timing, economics, trust, data, or workflow structure.
3. Names limits of transfer and avoids treating a famous example as proof.
4. Converts the precedent into a testable hypothesis rather than a copied solution.
5. In the follow-up, can explain why an attractive precedent fails because the underlying system differs.

## Warning Signs

1. Uses analogies as decoration: "Company X did it, so we should too."
2. Confuses surface similarity with structural similarity.
3. Cannot name what would need to be true for the precedent to apply.
4. Has no test plan before importing the idea into a different context.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Finds a meaningful analogy, decomposes why it applies, names transfer limits, and turns the borrowed pattern into a testable product hypothesis.
- **3:** Offers a plausible precedent, but the extraction, limits, or test plan need prompting.
- **1:** Copies a surface-level example with no reasoning about fit, constraints, or evidence.

## Role Or Level Notes

For senior candidates, expect a broader search space: adjacent industries, operational systems, marketplaces, regulated domains, enterprise workflows, or historical patterns. Strong answers should show both imagination and restraint.
