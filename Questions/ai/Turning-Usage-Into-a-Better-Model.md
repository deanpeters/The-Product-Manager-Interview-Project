# Turning Usage Into a Better Model

> Tell me about a time you used real usage data, evaluations, or feedback to actually change how an AI feature behaved — not just report on it. What changed?

> **Follow-up** How do you know the change was actually an improvement, and not just different?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning

## AI Competency

[AI Product Operations & Learning](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-product-operations--learning)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). An AI product's real advantage often comes from closing the loop between production behavior and future improvement — prompt changes, retrieval tuning, fine-tuning, or guardrail adjustments driven by real evaluation, not just intuition. This question tests whether a candidate has actually done that, and whether they can tell real improvement from mere change.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What signal told you something needed to change — a metric, a complaint pattern, a manual review?
- What exactly did you change — the prompt, the retrieval, the model, a guardrail?
- What would have told you the change made things worse instead of better?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific real signal (not just "user feedback" vaguely) that triggered the change.
2. Describes the actual mechanism changed — specific enough to show real hands-on understanding of how the system works.
3. Describes a real before/after comparison method, not just a subjective sense that things got better.
4. Shows awareness that a change could have made things worse, and how that would have been caught.

## Warning Signs

1. "Improvement" is asserted with no comparison method.
2. Vague about what was actually changed in the system.
3. No consideration that the change could have had a negative effect.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific signal, specific mechanism changed, a real before/after comparison, and awareness of how a negative result would have been caught.
- **3:** A real change is described but the comparison method or the specificity of the mechanism is thin.
- **1:** No real signal or mechanism — improvement is just asserted.
