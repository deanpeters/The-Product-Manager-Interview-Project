# How Much Should the AI Decide

> Tell me about a product decision where you had to decide how much a human should stay in the loop versus letting the AI act on its own. How did you draw that line?

> **Follow-up** What would have to change for you to move that line — give the AI more autonomy, or less?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## AI Competency

[AI Experience & Human Agency](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-experience--human-agency)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). Deciding how much agency to hand to an AI system — full automation, human-in-the-loop review, or something in between — is a real, consequential product decision, not a UI detail. This question tests whether a candidate can articulate a real reasoning process for that line rather than defaulting to maximum automation or maximum caution.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What was the cost of getting that line wrong in each direction — too much automation, or too much friction from human review?
- How did you signal the AI's confidence level to the user, if at all?
- What would an escalation path look like when the AI wasn't sure?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific real decision with real stakes on both sides — cost of over-automating and cost of over-gating.
2. Explains the actual reasoning for where the line was drawn, tied to consequence and reversibility of the AI's action, not an arbitrary default.
3. Describes a real mechanism for signaling confidence or uncertainty to the user, or an escalation path for low-confidence cases.
4. In the follow-up, gives specific, real conditions that would move the line — not just "if it got better."

## Warning Signs

1. Defaults to "always keep a human in the loop" or "just let the AI decide" with no situational reasoning.
2. No consideration of the cost of the choice in the other direction.
3. No real escalation or confidence-signaling mechanism described.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific real decision, reasoning tied to consequence and reversibility, a real confidence/escalation mechanism, and specific conditions for moving the line.
- **3:** A real decision exists but the reasoning is somewhat generic or the escalation mechanism is vague.
- **1:** No real decision, or a reflexive default with no situational reasoning.
