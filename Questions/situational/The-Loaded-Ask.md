# The Loaded Ask

> A stakeholder sends you an urgent message: "We need X by Friday or we lose the deal." Walk me through how you'd actually respond in the next hour.

> **Follow-up** Now tell me about a real message like that you've received. What was the literal ask, and what was the actual underlying need?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## Why This Matters

Urgent, loaded messages are one of the most common ways a PM gets pulled into building the wrong thing under time pressure. This question tests whether a candidate can separate the literal ask from the underlying need in real time, without either capitulating immediately or stonewalling a genuinely urgent situation.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would you actually do in that first hour — who would you talk to, and what would you ask them?
- How would you tell the difference between real urgency and someone else's manufactured deadline?
- In the real example, what did you end up doing, and how did it land with the person who sent the message?

## Strong Evidence

1. In the scenario, describes concrete first steps — a specific question to ask the stakeholder, a way to check the deadline's reality — rather than either "I'd just build it" or "I'd push back immediately."
2. Distinguishes what would make the urgency genuine from what would make it manufactured, and explains how they'd tell the difference quickly.
3. In the real example, clearly separates the literal ask from the underlying need, with specifics.
4. Describes how the resolution actually landed with the requester — not just that a good decision was made, but that the relationship survived it.

## Warning Signs

1. Scenario answer is purely reactive — building immediately or refusing immediately, with no investigation step.
2. No real distinction offered between genuine and manufactured urgency.
3. Real example is vague or doesn't actually show a gap between the literal ask and the underlying need.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Concrete, fast investigation steps in the scenario, a clear way to test urgency, and a real example with a well-articulated ask-vs-need gap and a preserved relationship.
- **3:** Reasonable scenario answer, but the real example is thinner or the urgency-testing method is vague.
- **1:** Purely reactive answer with no investigation, or no real example offered.
