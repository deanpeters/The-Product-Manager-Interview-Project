# A Confident Opinion With No Evidence

> A powerful, politically well-connected stakeholder pushes a strong opinion with zero supporting evidence, and you know pushing back risks an escalation that could cost you. What do you actually do?

> **Follow-up** Tell me about a real time this happened — how did you make them earn their case?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication
- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## Why This Matters

An arrogant, evidence-free opinion is easy to identify from the outside; it's much harder to actually challenge when the person holding it has real political power to make your life difficult. This question tests whether a candidate can hold a line on evidence without either capitulating to avoid the conflict or picking a fight they can't win. See [negotiation-signals.md](../../docs/negotiation-signals.md) for the specific "going to the balcony" and labeling techniques worth listening for here.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specifically did you ask them to provide?
- Whose voices, if any, did you bring into the conversation to broaden it beyond just you and them?
- Did you try to redirect their idea toward something aligned with the product strategy, rather than just opposing it outright?

## Strong Evidence

1. Asks the stakeholder directly for evidence rather than avoiding the confrontation entirely.
2. Brings their own evidence — and ideally more than one voice — rather than relying solely on their own authority to counter the stakeholder's.
3. Looks for a way to redirect the underlying idea toward something evidence-aligned, rather than only fighting it head-on.
4. In the follow-up, gives a real, specific example with a real resolution — not a hypothetical.
5. Bonus signal: describes deliberately staying calm and stepping back before responding ("going to the balcony") rather than reacting in the moment, or names the stakeholder's underlying concern before disputing their conclusion. See [negotiation-signals.md](../../docs/negotiation-signals.md).

## Warning Signs

1. Capitulates to avoid political risk, with no real attempt to ask for evidence.
2. Picks a pure confrontation with no evidence-gathering step of their own.
3. No real example in the follow-up, or the example has no real resolution.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Directly asks for evidence, brings real counter-evidence and additional voices, attempts a redirect rather than pure opposition, and has a specific real example with a real resolution.
- **3:** Some pushback occurs but is thin on evidence-gathering, or the real example is vague.
- **1:** Capitulates entirely, or picks a fight with no evidence brought to it.
