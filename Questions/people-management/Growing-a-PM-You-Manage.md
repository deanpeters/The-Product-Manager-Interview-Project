# Growing a PM You Manage

> Tell me about a specific PM you coached whose judgment measurably improved while working for you. What did you actually do?

> **Follow-up** Tell me about a time you had to give that person — or another report — hard feedback. How did you approach it, and how did it land?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## People Management Competency

[Coaching & Development](../../docs/people-management-competency.md#coaching--development)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles that manage other PMs — see [people-management-competency.md](../../docs/people-management-competency.md). Coaching is easy to claim and hard to demonstrate; "I mentor my team" is a platitude until it's attached to a specific person and a specific, observable change in how they think. This question also tests whether a candidate can deliver hard feedback directly, not just encouragement.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specifically changed in how that person approached their work?
- How do you know it was your coaching and not just their own growth over time?
- What was the hardest piece of feedback you had to give them, word for word if you can?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific person and a specific, observable change in their judgment or output — not a vague "they grew a lot."
2. Describes the actual coaching mechanism used — regular structured 1:1s, a specific technique, real-time feedback on decisions — not just "I was available."
3. Shows some humility about attribution — acknowledges the person's own growth alongside their own coaching, rather than taking full credit.
4. In the follow-up, describes real hard feedback with real content, and an honest account of how it landed (not just "they took it well").

## Warning Signs

1. No specific person or change named — stays at the level of general coaching philosophy.
2. Takes full credit for another person's growth with no acknowledgment of their own effort.
3. In the follow-up, the "hard feedback" is actually mild, or the account of how it landed is implausibly positive.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific person, specific observable change, a real coaching mechanism, and a genuine hard-feedback account with an honest outcome.
- **3:** A real coaching relationship is described but the change or the hard-feedback account is vague.
- **1:** No specific person or change, or feedback in the follow-up isn't actually hard.
