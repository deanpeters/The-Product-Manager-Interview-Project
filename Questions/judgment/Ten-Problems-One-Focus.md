# Ten Problems, One Focus

> Your product team has identified ten customer problems it could address. Walk me through how you'd narrow that down — without simply plugging numbers into a prioritization formula.

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Prioritization frameworks (RICE, ICE, and similar scoring models) are easy to recite and easy to game — most candidates can name one. What's harder, and more revealing, is whether a candidate can reason about trade-offs explicitly: what they're optimizing for, what they're knowingly giving up, and why a formula alone isn't sufficient.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What information would you actually want about each of the ten before narrowing anything?
- What would you optimize for, and what would that choice cost you?
- Where would a scoring framework mislead you here, if anywhere?
- How would you bring the team along on a call that some of them will disagree with?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a real reasoning process — clustering related problems, weighing evidence strength and reach, considering strategic fit — rather than only naming a framework.
2. Explicitly names what they'd optimize for and what gets sacrificed as a result, showing they understand prioritization as trade-off, not just ranking.
3. Identifies a specific way a formula alone could mislead (e.g., easily-gamed confidence scores, ignoring strategic sequencing, ignoring dependencies between problems).
4. Addresses how they'd bring a team along on a decision that disappoints some stakeholders.

## Warning Signs

1. Answer is just a framework name with no reasoning about this specific situation.
2. No mention of what's being sacrificed — prioritization framed as pure addition with no real trade-off.
3. No awareness of the limits of a scoring formula.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Substantive reasoning process, explicit trade-off language, a specific critique of formula-only prioritization, and a real plan for bringing the team along.
- **3:** Names a reasonable process but leans heavily on a framework name without much situational reasoning.
- **1:** Framework name only, with no real reasoning about this specific set of ten problems.
