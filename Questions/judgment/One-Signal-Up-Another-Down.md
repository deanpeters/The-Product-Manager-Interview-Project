# One Signal Up, Another Down

> Adoption of a feature you own is climbing, but retention on the same feature is falling. Both numbers are real. Walk me through how you'd investigate what's actually happening — before you decide what, if anything, to do about it.

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning
- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

This tests live diagnostic reasoning, not framework recall or after-the-fact reflection. [Vanity Metrics vs. Actionable Analytics](Vanity-Analytics_vs_Action-Metrics.md) tests whether a candidate can name what makes a metric trustworthy; [Shipped, But Didn't Work](Shipped-But-Didnt-Work.md) tests how they process a bet that's already resolved as a failure. Neither tests what this does: two real signals disagreeing with each other, no verdict yet, and the candidate has to build a hypothesis and a plan to test it rather than pattern-match to a familiar story. A candidate who resolves the tension too quickly — picking whichever number supports the answer they wanted — is showing exactly the failure mode this card is built to surface.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What's your first hypothesis for why these two numbers could move in opposite directions at once?
- What would you look at to test that hypothesis, beyond the two topline numbers?
- How would you segment the data to see if this is one story or several different stories mixed together?
- Suppose your first hypothesis turns out to be wrong — what's your next move?
- How would you decide when you have enough evidence to act, versus when you need to keep investigating?

## Strong Evidence

1. Generates more than one plausible hypothesis before committing to an explanation (e.g., a new-user spike diluting retention, a UX change that helps first use but hurts repeat use, a segment-specific effect).
2. Proposes a concrete way to test a hypothesis — segmentation, cohort analysis, qualitative follow-up — rather than staring at the two topline numbers.
3. Resists the urge to declare a verdict before the evidence supports one; distinguishes "here's a plausible story" from "here's what's actually happening."
4. Connects the investigation to a decision — what they'd do differently depending on which hypothesis holds up.

## Warning Signs

1. Picks one explanation immediately and doesn't consider alternatives.
2. Treats the two numbers as if only one of them is real ("adoption is what matters, ignore retention" or vice versa) without justifying why.
3. No plan to actually test the hypothesis — the investigation stops at "I'd look into it."
4. Jumps to a fix before establishing what's actually causing the divergence.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Generates multiple hypotheses, proposes a specific way to test them, explicitly resists premature conclusions, and ties the investigation to what they'd decide next.
- **3:** Reasonable single hypothesis and a plausible way to check it, but doesn't consider alternative explanations unprompted.
- **1:** Picks a story and sticks with it, or dismisses one of the two signals outright, with no real investigation plan.
