# Leading Through a Bad Quarter

> Tell me about a genuinely hard stretch for your team — a bad quarter, a layoff, a failed bet — and how you led through it.

> **Follow-up** What did you actually say to the team, and what did you deliberately not say?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## People Management Competency

[Leadership & Motivation](../../docs/people-management-competency.md#leadership--motivation)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles that manage other PMs — see [people-management-competency.md](../../docs/people-management-competency.md). Anyone can lead when things are going well. This question tests whether a candidate can sustain a team's morale and trust through a genuinely hard period without either sugarcoating reality (which erodes trust when the team finds out) or letting the team spiral (which erodes performance and retention).

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- How honest were you able to be about the situation, and where did you draw the line?
- What did the team's morale actually look like a month later?
- Did anyone leave, or almost leave, during or after this period? How did you handle that?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a genuinely hard, specific situation — not a mild inconvenience dressed up as adversity.
2. Gives real content for what they actually said to the team — specific language or approach, not "I stayed positive."
3. Names something they deliberately didn't say or share, showing real judgment about honesty versus what the team could productively act on.
4. Gives an honest account of the team's state afterward, including any real damage (attrition, morale dips) rather than an implausibly clean recovery.

## Warning Signs

1. The "hard stretch" described isn't actually hard.
2. No real content for what was said — just "I was transparent and supportive."
3. Implausibly positive outcome with no acknowledgment of any real cost.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Genuinely hard situation, real specific communication content, a real boundary on what wasn't shared, and an honest account of the aftermath including any real cost.
- **3:** A real hard period is described but the communication specifics or the honest aftermath are thin.
- **1:** Not actually a hard situation, or an implausibly clean account with no real difficulty.
