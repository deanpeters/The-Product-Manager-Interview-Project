# Catching Your Own Bias

> Tell me about a real product decision where you noticed — in yourself or a teammate — a cognitive bias like confirmation bias, sunk cost, or overconfidence actively shaping the call. What did you do about it?

> **Follow-up** What habits do you use to catch this before it costs you, not just after?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making
- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Cognitive bias is hard to catch precisely because it's invisible from the inside — confirmation bias feels like being right, and sunk-cost thinking feels like persistence. This question tests genuine self-awareness: can a candidate name the specific bias, recognize it in themselves (not just in others), and describe a real corrective action rather than a vague resolution to "be more objective."

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specifically made you notice it — what tipped you off?
- What did you actually do differently as a result, in that moment?
- Is this a recurring pattern for you, or a one-time catch?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names the specific bias accurately and applies it correctly to the situation described.
2. Includes a real instance of catching it in themselves, not only in a teammate or stakeholder.
3. Describes a concrete corrective action — seeking outside input, running a smaller test, deliberately slowing down — not just a vague intention.
4. Names a systemic habit that helps catch this going forward, not only a one-off save.

## Warning Signs

1. No specific bias named — stays at the level of "I try to stay objective."
2. Only describes catching bias in other people, never in themselves.
3. No concrete corrective action — the story ends at "I noticed it."

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific bias named accurately, a real instance of self-catching, a concrete corrective action, and a systemic habit that helps going forward.
- **3:** A real instance is described but the bias naming is loose or the corrective action is vague.
- **1:** No specific bias, or bias is only ever described in others.
