# Influence Without Authority

> Tell me about an important product decision where you didn't have authority over the people whose support you needed.

> **Follow-up** Now tell me about a serious disagreement with an engineer or designer. What was it actually about, and what happened?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## Why This Matters

PMs get things done through people they don't manage. This question tests whether a candidate can produce alignment through evidence and relationship rather than positional authority, and the follow-up specifically probes how they handle real disagreement with close creative/technical partners — not just polite alignment.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What was the actual source of disagreement — priorities, evidence, or something else?
- How did you make your case, and what made the difference in bringing people along?
- Did you change your mind at any point, or only the other person?
- What was the relationship like with that person afterward?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific situation with a named stakeholder group and what they needed from that group.
2. Explains the actual mechanism of influence used — evidence, a prototype, a shared framing of the problem — not just "I explained it well."
3. In the disagreement story, accurately characterizes what the disagreement was really about, showing they understood the other person's position, not just their own.
4. Shows willingness to have genuinely changed their own mind, not just persuaded the other party.

## Warning Signs

1. Influence story is really about using positional authority or escalation, not persuasion.
2. Disagreement story is one-sided — the other person is simply "wrong," with no credit given to their reasoning.
3. No account of the relationship afterward, suggesting the disagreement may not have been genuinely resolved.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific influence mechanism described, disagreement story shows real understanding of the other side, and evidence of intellectual honesty (willingness to be moved).
- **3:** Real examples exist but influence tactics or the disagreement account are somewhat one-dimensional.
- **1:** Relies on authority rather than influence, or the disagreement account is dismissive of the other person.
