# The First 90 Days

> You're starting a new PM role next Monday — new company, new product, new team. Walk me through what you'd actually do in the first 90 days, beyond "listen and learn."

> **Follow-up** What's something you'd deliberately avoid doing in that window, even if you had a good idea?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

"Listen and learn" is true but empty — every candidate says it. This question tests whether a candidate has a real, structured way of building context fast (customers, product, business, team) and, just as important, the discipline to resist proposing changes before they've earned the standing and the evidence to make them.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specifically would you do in week one versus week eight?
- Who would you talk to first, and why them specifically?
- How would you know when you'd learned enough to start proposing changes?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a real, sequenced approach — not just "meet people" but a specific plan for building product, customer, and business context.
2. Names specific people or sources of information they'd prioritize early, with reasoning for the order.
3. Names a real self-imposed restraint — a type of change they'd deliberately hold off on — not just a platitude about "not moving too fast."
4. Has a real signal for when they've learned enough to start acting, not an arbitrary timeline alone.

## Warning Signs

1. Generic "listen and learn" with no concrete plan behind it.
2. No real restraint named — implies they'd start making changes immediately based on early impressions.
3. No real signal for when discovery ends and action starts.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific sequenced plan, real reasoning for who/what comes first, a genuine self-imposed restraint, and a real signal for when to start acting.
- **3:** A plausible plan exists but is generic, or the restraint/signal is vague.
- **1:** "Listen and learn" with no real specifics.
