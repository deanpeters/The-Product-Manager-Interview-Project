# Deciding Before You're Certain

> Tell me about a consequential product decision you had to make before you had all the information you wanted.

> **Follow-up** How did you decide when you knew enough — as opposed to waiting for more data, when waiting also had a cost?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Product decisions almost never come with complete information, and waiting for certainty has its own cost. This question tests whether a candidate can reason explicitly about that trade-off rather than either freezing in analysis or barreling ahead on instinct alone.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What information did you not have, and how much did that actually matter?
- What would have changed your decision if you'd learned it later?
- Looking back, did you decide at the right time, too early, or too late?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names the specific missing information and explains why it was hard or costly to obtain in time.
2. Articulates an explicit reasoning process for when they had "enough" evidence to act, not just a gut call.
3. Acknowledges the risk they were accepting by deciding early, and what they did to mitigate it (e.g., a reversible choice, a smaller first bet).
4. Reflects honestly on whether the timing was right in hindsight.

## Warning Signs

1. Describes a decision that wasn't actually made under real uncertainty — a low-stakes or already-obvious call.
2. No articulated reasoning for the timing — just "I went with my gut."
3. No acknowledgment of risk or a mitigation strategy; pure confidence with no hedge.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Real high-stakes example, explicit reasoning about the data/waiting trade-off, a named mitigation for the uncertainty accepted, and honest hindsight reflection.
- **3:** A real decision under some uncertainty, but the reasoning about timing is thin or implicit.
- **1:** No genuine uncertainty in the example, or no visible reasoning about when to decide.
