# Interview Scorecard Template

## Purpose

A fillable, per-round worksheet an interviewer completes during or immediately after their round of the [core interview loop](core-interview-loop.md). It operationalizes [scoring-rubric.md](scoring-rubric.md) and [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](evidence-and-pressure-standard.md) into something you actually fill out live, rather than reconstruct from memory before a debrief.

Copy the template below once per interviewer per candidate. Fill it during the interview if you can, or immediately after — evidence recorded from memory an hour later is worse evidence.

## How To Use This

1. Fill the header before the interview starts.
2. As the candidate answers, log evidence under each question you actually ask — a quote or close paraphrase, not a summary of your impression.
3. After the interview, score each competency your round owns against [scoring-rubric.md](scoring-rubric.md), independently of what you think other interviewers will say.
4. Compare your scores to the level benchmark table below for the role's target level (or the AI-PM / People Management benchmark tables, if you ran the optional Round 5 or Round 6) — a score below the benchmark isn't automatically disqualifying, but it's a specific thing to raise in the debrief with evidence, not just a gut flag.
5. Submit before any debrief conversation. Don't let a hallway comment from another interviewer shift your score before you've written it down.

## Level Benchmark Reference

A starting point for what score a competency should clear at each target level, adapted from an internal PM self-assessment worksheet in this repository's `Research/` folder (`Research/Product Manager Assessment - December 11, 2021.csv`). These are a reasonable default, not a validated standard — calibrate them against your own org's actual bar before treating them as a pass/fail line. Note that the expectation for a competency doesn't always increase monotonically with level; some (like hands-on customer discovery) matter most in the middle of the ladder and get delegated more at the top, same as in the source worksheet.

| Competency | PM | Senior PM | Director | VP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Customer Understanding And Discovery | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 |
| Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 |
| Delivery, Execution, And Ownership | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| Influence, Collaboration, And Communication | 3 | 4 | 5 | 5 |
| Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 |

A score meaningfully below the benchmark for the target level is worth raising explicitly in the debrief with the specific evidence behind it — not as an automatic reject, but as a named gap the group should discuss rather than let get averaged away by other rounds.

## Round Recommendation

- **Overall signal for this round:** (Strong / Solid / Mixed / Weak — describe in evidence terms, not a single adjective alone)
- **Notes for the debrief:** (anything the group needs to know before comparing scores — a concern that didn't get resolved, a question that ran short on time, a pressure-level note that applied)

## What This Isn't

This scorecard is a per-round worksheet, not a hiring decision. The [structured interview guide](structured-interview-guide.md) covers how independent round scores come together in a debrief. Don't use this template to produce a single blended number per candidate — that collapses the exact evidence-per-competency discipline the rest of this toolkit is built around.
