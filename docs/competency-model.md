# Competency Model

## Purpose

This document defines what this repository's interview cards assess. It replaces the old five-item checklist (Product Design, Product Strategy, Analytical, Technical, Cultural Fit) with the competency set named in the [README](../README.md#what-we-assess) and grounded in [the research](../Research/). Every interview card should map to one or more competencies below rather than inventing new labels.

The old checklist is not deleted from history — it is superseded. Legacy question files may still carry it; new and rewritten cards use this model instead.

## The Competencies

### Customer Understanding And Discovery
How a candidate learns what customers actually need, distinguishes stated preferences from underlying problems, and brings evidence rather than assumption into decisions.

**Look for:** direct customer contact, specific discovery methods, examples of a belief that changed after talking to customers, ability to separate a customer's request from their underlying job or pain.

### Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
How a candidate reasons about what to build, in what order, and why — under real constraints rather than in the abstract.

**Look for:** explicit trade-offs (not just "we prioritized X"), a defensible prioritization logic, awareness of opportunity cost, ability to say what they chose *not* to do and why.

### Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning
How a candidate defines success, chooses and interprets metrics, and updates their approach based on results — including results that contradicted their plan.

**Look for:** distinguishing actionable metrics from vanity metrics, specific numbers and sources, honest description of a metric or bet that didn't pan out, what they changed as a result.

### Delivery, Execution, And Ownership
How a candidate gets things shipped: working with engineering and design, managing scope and risk, and taking responsibility for outcomes rather than just output.

**Look for:** concrete delivery mechanics, how they handled a slip or scope cut, evidence of following through past the "fun part" of a project, ownership language ("I decided," "I was accountable for") rather than only "we."

### Influence, Collaboration, And Communication
How a candidate works with people they don't manage, handles disagreement, and communicates decisions and their reasoning to different audiences.

**Look for:** examples of influencing without authority, how they handled a stakeholder who disagreed, tailoring communication to technical vs. non-technical audiences, evidence of genuinely incorporating another person's input rather than just informing them.

### Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making
How a candidate handles situations with a real ethical dimension — user trust, data use, honest communication about risk or failure — and how honestly they reflect on their own role in an outcome.

**Look for:** a real example rather than a hypothetical platitude, willingness to describe a mistake or a decision they'd make differently now, reasoning that weighs user or societal impact rather than only business metrics.

## Using This Model

- Each interview card should name which competencies it primarily assesses (see the [interview-card template](../Questions/Interview%20Question%20Boilerplate.md)).
- No single question should try to cover all six competencies; a card that claims to assess everything usually assesses nothing well.
- When assembling an interview loop, cover the full set across interviewers rather than repeating the same competency in every slot — see [structured-interview-guide.md](structured-interview-guide.md).
- Score each competency independently using [scoring-rubric.md](scoring-rubric.md) rather than forming one overall gut impression.
