# The Business Case Behind the Ask

> Tell me about a time you had to build the business case for something you wanted funded — a pilot, a feature, a new bet. What was the weakest part of your case, and what happened when someone found it?

> **Follow-up** What alternative did you have to seriously compare against, rather than wave away?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning

## Why This Matters

Anyone can build a business case that looks good from the front. This question tests whether a candidate can honestly identify the weakest part of their own case — which requires real self-awareness about their evidence — and how they handled someone else finding that same weakness. It also tests whether "alternatives considered" was a real comparison or a formality.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Was the weak part something you knew about going in, or something you only realized when challenged?
- What did you do once the weakness was exposed — defend it, patch it, or concede the point?
- How seriously did you actually evaluate the alternative in the follow-up — what would have had to be true for you to choose it instead?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific, real weakness in their own case, not a token one ("we didn't have perfect data" as filler doesn't count).
2. Shows honest reflection on whether they knew about the weakness beforehand or were caught off guard.
3. Describes a real response to the challenge — conceding, patching with new evidence, or defending with a specific reason — rather than deflecting.
4. In the follow-up, describes a genuine alternative with real trade-offs, not a strawman they could easily beat.

## Warning Signs

1. Claims the case had no real weakness — defensiveness rather than honest reflection.
2. The "alternative considered" is obviously inferior and wasn't a real comparison.
3. No account of what happened when the weakness was found — story ends before the hard part.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific, honestly-named weakness, a real account of the challenge and response, and a genuine alternative comparison in the follow-up.
- **3:** A real business case is described, but the weakness or the alternative comparison feels softened.
- **1:** No real weakness admitted, or the alternative was never a serious option.

## Role Or Level Notes

For candidates pitching this repository's own interview loop or similar internal tooling decisions, this question also works as a light self-referential check — but keep the example role-relevant; don't force a mismatch.
