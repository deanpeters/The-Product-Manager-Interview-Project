# A Critique You'd Stand Behind

> Pick a product you genuinely use and respect. Tell me one thing about it you'd change, and why you think the team behind it probably hasn't fixed it already.

> **Follow-up** Now do the opposite: pick a product you think is overrated. What is it actually getting right that its critics miss?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

It's easy to criticize a product you dislike and easy to praise one you like uncritically. This question is harder in both directions: finding a real flaw in something you respect, and finding real merit in something you're inclined to dismiss. Both require setting aside a first reaction and reasoning about trade-offs the actual team was likely facing.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Why do you think the team hasn't already fixed the thing you'd change — what constraint or trade-off might explain it?
- Is there a version of "fixing" it that could make something else worse?
- For the overrated product: what evidence makes you think it's actually working, rather than just guessing at the team's reasoning?

## Strong Evidence

1. Picks a specific, real product they actually use, not a generic industry example.
2. The critique is specific and shows genuine use, not a surface-level complaint.
3. Offers a plausible reason the team hasn't fixed it — a trade-off, a constraint, a different priority — showing empathy for the original decision rather than assuming incompetence.
4. In the follow-up, finds genuine, specific merit in the "overrated" product rather than a backhanded compliment.

## Warning Signs

1. Critique is generic or clearly rehearsed ("the onboarding could be better") with no specific detail.
2. Assumes the team simply didn't think of the obvious fix, with no consideration of what trade-off might explain it.
3. The "overrated" product answer stays purely negative — no genuine credit given.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific, well-reasoned critique with real empathy for the original trade-off, and genuine, specific credit given to the "overrated" product.
- **3:** Reasonable critique and counter-example, but empathy for the original decision or genuine credit in the follow-up is thin.
- **1:** Generic critique with no real specificity, or a purely negative "overrated" answer with no real merit acknowledged.
