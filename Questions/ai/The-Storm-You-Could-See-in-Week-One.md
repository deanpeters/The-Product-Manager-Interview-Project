# The Storm You Could See in Week One

> Tell me about an AI project that ran into serious trouble months in — a stalled launch, a scrapped feature, a credibility hit with leadership. Looking back, was the root cause something that was actually knowable at kickoff — a misread of the mission, a misread of the organization's real risk appetite, data that wasn't ready, or infrastructure that wasn't there — or was it something that genuinely couldn't have been seen coming?

> **Follow-up** If it was knowable at kickoff, why didn't it get caught then?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## AI Competency

[AI Risk, Safety & Responsible Use](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-risk-safety--responsible-use)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). "Scanning External Forces Before You Bet on AI" tests a candidate's read on external, regulatory, and competitive risk. This card tests the complementary internal lens: whether a candidate can honestly trace an AI project's failure back to an internal condition — a misunderstood mandate, an underestimated organizational appetite for risk, a data-readiness gap, or an infrastructure gap — that was actually visible at the start, rather than only becoming visible once the project had already failed. The goal isn't blame; it's whether the candidate does real post-mortem reasoning instead of attributing failure to bad luck or factors genuinely outside anyone's control.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specifically would have had to be different at kickoff for this to have been caught?
- Who would have needed to raise it, and what made that hard to do at the time?
- What do you do differently now at the start of an AI project as a result of this?
- Was there a moment mid-project where it could still have been course-corrected, and was it?

## Strong Evidence

1. Identifies a specific, plausible root cause among mission misread, risk-appetite misread, data readiness, or infrastructure gap — not a vague "things went wrong."
2. Honestly acknowledges the cause was knowable at kickoff, if that's the truth, rather than reframing it as unforeseeable to protect their own record.
3. Explains why it wasn't caught at the time — a real organizational or process reason, not just "we missed it."
4. Describes a concrete change to how they now run AI project kickoffs as a result.

## Warning Signs

1. Attributes the failure entirely to external or unforeseeable factors when a knowable internal cause is actually more plausible.
2. No real explanation for why a knowable risk wasn't caught — just restates that it wasn't.
3. No behavior change described — the lesson stays abstract with no effect on how they work now.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific root cause honestly identified as knowable at kickoff, a real explanation for why it was missed, and a concrete change to current practice as a result.
- **3:** Real project and real cause named, but thin on why it was missed or vague about what's changed since.
- **1:** Attributes failure to bad luck or unforeseeable circumstance when a knowable cause was more plausible, with no real self-examination.
