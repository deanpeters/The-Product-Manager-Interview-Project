# People Management Competency Model

## Purpose

An optional, additional competency layer for roles that manage other PMs — Director, VP, or Head of Product roles where the job includes hiring, developing, and leading a team, not just doing individual-contributor product work. It sits alongside, not instead of, the six core competencies in [competency-model.md](competency-model.md): a person can be an excellent individual-contributor PM and still be untested on whether they can grow one.

**Don't use this for individual-contributor PM or Senior PM roles.** Testing people-management judgment on a candidate who won't manage anyone tests a skill the role doesn't need. Reserve this layer for roles where managing PMs is a real, named part of the job.

The five categories below, and their level benchmarks, are drawn from the "People Skills And Related Competencies" section of [`Research/Product Manager Assessment - December 11, 2021.csv`](../Research/Product%20Manager%20Assessment%20-%20December%2011%2C%202021.csv) — the same internal worksheet that informed the core [scorecard's level-benchmark table](interview-scorecard-template.md). Note that several of these competencies are scored 1 (essentially not applicable) at the individual-contributor PM level in that worksheet, which is exactly why this layer is gated to manager-track roles.

## The Categories

### Coaching & Development
Growing another PM's judgment and skill over time — not doing their thinking for them, but building their capability to reason well on their own.

**Look for:** a specific person they coached, a specific change in that person's judgment or output over time, and real feedback given (including hard feedback), not just encouragement.

### Hiring & Team Composition
Recruiting, evaluating, and onboarding PMs, and shaping the team's overall skill mix and structure — including decisions about what *not* to hire for.

**Look for:** a specific hiring or team-structure decision with real reasoning, including a case where they decided not to fill a role or hired for a different profile than the obvious one.

### Evangelism
Getting an audience much larger than the immediate team — an org, a company, an executive layer — to genuinely internalize a product vision, not just hear it announced once.

**Look for:** a specific vision or strategy that had to travel beyond the room where it was written, and evidence it actually changed how other people made decisions, not just that it was communicated.

### Leadership & Motivation
Sustaining a team's morale, engagement, and psychological safety — especially through a hard stretch (a bad quarter, a layoff, a failed bet) — without either sugarcoating reality or letting the team spiral.

**Look for:** a specific hard period, what they actually said and did (not just "I stayed positive"), and a real, honest account of the team's state afterward.

### Organization Design
Structuring a team — reporting lines, team boundaries, how PMs are matched to product areas — to fit the current state of the business, and adjusting that structure as the business changes.

**Look for:** a specific structural decision (a team split, a merge, a reporting-line change) with real reasoning tied to a business need, not an org chart preference.

## Using This Model

Each people-management card lives in [`Questions/people-management/`](../Questions/people-management/) and names both its category (from this document) and its underlying core competency (from [competency-model.md](competency-model.md)) — see [question-index.md](question-index.md) for the full listing. Use these as an optional additional round for manager-track loops only — see [core-interview-loop.md](core-interview-loop.md) Round 6 and [role-loop-presets.md](role-loop-presets.md)'s Director/VP preset.
