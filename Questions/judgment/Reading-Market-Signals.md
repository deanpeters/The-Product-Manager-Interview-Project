# Reading Market Signals

> Tell me about a time understanding your customers' alternatives — what they'd use instead of your product — changed your product strategy.

> **Follow-up** Tell me about a market or competitor signal that caused you to reconsider where your product should compete.

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

Strategy that only looks inward at the product's own roadmap misses the market context that actually determines whether a differentiation matters. This question tests whether a candidate thinks in terms of competitive alternatives and market signals, not just feature lists.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What were customers actually using before or instead of your product?
- What specifically changed in your strategy as a result, and how did you know it was the right change?
- What made the competitor or market signal credible enough to act on, rather than noise?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific alternative customers were using — a competitor, a manual process, a substitute — not a vague "the market."
2. Describes a concrete strategic change that followed, not just an observation.
3. Shows judgment about which signals were worth acting on versus noise — a specific reason the signal was credible.
4. Connects the change back to a customer or business outcome.

## Warning Signs

1. "Strategy change" is really a feature addition with no real repositioning.
2. No account of how they distinguished a real signal from noise.
3. Answer is abstract industry commentary rather than a specific personal decision.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific alternative or signal named, a real strategic change described, and clear reasoning for why the signal was credible and worth acting on.
- **3:** A real example exists but the strategic change or the credibility reasoning is thin.
- **1:** No specific example, or the answer stays at the level of general market commentary.

## Role Or Level Notes

For a Director/VP-level candidate, add: "How did you tell the difference between a competitor genuinely funding that direction and a competitor just announcing it?" — see [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](../../docs/evidence-and-pressure-standard.md) for what a well-grounded answer looks like versus one built on a press release alone.
