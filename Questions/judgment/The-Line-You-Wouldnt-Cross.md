# The Line You Wouldn't Cross

> Tell me about an AI capability you or your team chose not to ship, or deliberately delayed, because of a risk — privacy, bias, misuse, or giving the AI too much autonomy. Walk me through the decision.

> **Follow-up** Who else needed to be part of that call, and why?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## AI Competency

[AI Risk, Safety & Responsible Use](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-risk-safety--responsible-use)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). AI capabilities routinely raise privacy, bias, security, and misuse risks that a feature roadmap alone won't surface. This question, paired conceptually with [When Growth Could Harm Customers](../judgment/When-Growth-Could-Harm-Customers.md), tests whether a candidate has actually held a line under real business pressure, not just claims to care about responsible AI in the abstract.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What was the real cost of not shipping or delaying — in time, revenue, or competitive position?
- How did you investigate the risk before deciding, rather than just asserting it?
- Did the decision hold, or did it get overridden later?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific real capability and a specific real risk category — not a vague "we were careful."
2. Describes actually investigating the risk (talking to legal, security, affected users, or running a smaller test) rather than deciding from instinct alone.
3. Names a real cost of the decision — this wasn't a free or easy call.
4. Names specific people or functions who were part of the decision, showing this wasn't a solo call on something consequential.

## Warning Signs

1. No specific capability or risk named — stays at the level of general principle.
2. No real investigation — the risk was asserted, not examined.
3. Frames the decision as costless, suggesting it wasn't actually a hard trade-off.
4. Solo decision on something that plausibly needed broader input.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific capability and risk, real investigation, a genuine cost accepted, and the right people involved in the decision.
- **3:** A real decision exists but the investigation or the cost accepted is unclear.
- **1:** No specific example, or the decision was made without real investigation or broader input.
