# What the Model Needs to Know

> Tell me about a time you had to figure out what data, context, or permissions an AI feature actually needed to work well. What did you get wrong about that at first?

> **Follow-up** How did you handle data you needed but didn't have, or couldn't get?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[Data & Context Strategy](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#data--context-strategy)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). An AI feature's quality is often bottlenecked by what data or context it can actually access, not by the model itself — a distinction candidates without hands-on AI product experience often miss. Asking what they got *wrong* at first, specifically, filters out rehearsed answers.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What made you realize the data or context assumption was wrong?
- Whose permission or cooperation did you need to get the right data, and how did that go?
- What did the fix actually look like — more data, better retrieval, a narrower scope?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific real gap between what data/context the feature needed and what was actually assumed or available at first.
2. Honestly describes what was wrong about the initial assumption, not just the eventual solution.
3. Describes the real mechanism used to close the gap — different retrieval, new data source, narrowed scope, permission negotiation — with enough specificity to show real hands-on work.
4. In the follow-up, gives a genuine account of handling a real data gap, including scoping the feature down if that was the honest answer.

## Warning Signs

1. No real gap admitted — describes a smooth process with no wrong initial assumption.
2. Answer stays abstract about "the right data" without specifics.
3. In the follow-up, no real account of what happens when the ideal data isn't available.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific real gap, honest account of the wrong initial assumption, a concrete fix, and a genuine account of handling missing data.
- **3:** A real example exists but the "what did you get wrong" part is soft or generic.
- **1:** No real gap, or the answer stays entirely abstract.
