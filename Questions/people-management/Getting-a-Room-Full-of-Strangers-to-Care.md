# Getting a Room Full of Strangers to Care

> Tell me about a time you had to get an organization much larger than your immediate team to genuinely internalize a product vision or strategy — not just hear it announced once.

> **Follow-up** How do you know it actually landed, versus people just nodding in the room?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## People Management Competency

[Evangelism](../../docs/people-management-competency.md#evangelism)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles that manage other PMs — see [people-management-competency.md](../../docs/people-management-competency.md). A vision that only lives in the room where it was written hasn't actually done its job. This question tests whether a candidate has real experience scaling a message beyond their immediate team, and — more importantly — whether they have a genuine way of knowing it actually changed behavior rather than just being politely received.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Who specifically did you need to reach, and how did you reach them?
- What did you repeat, and how many times, before it actually stuck?
- What's a specific decision someone made later that showed the vision had actually landed?

## Strong Evidence

1. Names a specific vision or strategy and a specific, real audience larger than their immediate team.
2. Describes a real mechanism for reaching that audience repeatedly, not a single announcement.
3. Gives a real, specific example of someone else's later decision or behavior that showed the message had actually landed.
4. Shows awareness that evangelism is repetitive work, not a one-time event.

## Warning Signs

1. Describes a single announcement or all-hands as if that alone constituted evangelism.
2. No real evidence the message actually changed anyone's behavior — just that it was said.
3. No account of repetition or reinforcement over time.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific vision, a real multi-touch mechanism for reaching a broad audience, and concrete evidence it changed someone else's later decision.
- **3:** A real communication effort is described but the evidence it landed is thin.
- **1:** A single announcement treated as sufficient, with no real evidence of impact.
