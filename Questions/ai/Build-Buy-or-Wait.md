# Build, Buy, or Wait

> Tell me about a build-vs-buy-vs-partner decision you made for an AI capability. What tipped the decision, and what did you get wrong about the cost or latency tradeoffs going in?

> **Follow-up** How did the total cost of the choice compare with what you first estimated?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## AI Competency

[AI Economics & Technical Tradeoffs](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-economics--technical-tradeoffs)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). Model choice and build/buy/partner decisions carry real, often underestimated cost and latency implications that compound at scale. This question tests whether a candidate has genuinely reasoned through that trade-off space, including being honest about where their initial estimate was wrong.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What specific factors tipped it toward build, buy, or partner?
- What would have made a different choice the right one?
- Was the miss on cost, latency, quality, or something else entirely?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a specific real decision with the actual factors weighed — cost, latency, quality, control, time to market — not just "we decided to build it."
2. Honestly names what was wrong about the initial cost or latency estimate, with real numbers or a real magnitude if possible.
3. Explains what would have made a different choice correct, showing the decision was a genuine trade-off, not an inevitability.
4. In the follow-up, gives a specific, honest account of the actual-versus-estimated gap.

## Warning Signs

1. No real trade-off reasoning — the choice is presented as obviously correct with no serious alternative considered.
2. No honest account of what was wrong about the initial estimate.
3. Follow-up reveals no real tracking of actual cost against the original estimate.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific real decision with genuine trade-off reasoning, an honestly named estimate miss, and a real actual-vs-estimated comparison in the follow-up.
- **3:** A real decision exists but the estimate-miss honesty or the trade-off reasoning is thin.
- **1:** No real trade-off considered, or no honest account of the estimate being wrong.
