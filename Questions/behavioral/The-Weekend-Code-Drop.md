# The Weekend Code Drop

> Tell me about a time a manager or senior technical leader outside your day-to-day work "swooped in" with a ready-made solution — code they wrote over the weekend, a fully formed idea — and expected you to just implement it.

> **Follow-up** What did you actually do with what they brought you?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## Why This Matters

A senior person dropping in with a ready-made solution usually comes from good intentions and real domain expertise — but accepted uncritically, it creates context-switching, bypasses the team's own process, and can produce a bolted-on result nobody actually owns. This question tests whether a candidate can turn that kind of drive-by input into real collaboration rather than either rubber-stamping it or dismissing it.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What did you ask them about the problem they were trying to solve?
- Did you end up using their exact solution, a modified version, or something different entirely — and why?
- Did anything change afterward in how often this happened?

## Strong Evidence

1. Treats the person as a partner with real insight, not an obstacle to route around.
2. Asks what problem they were actually trying to solve, rather than just evaluating the solution as handed over.
3. Describes folding in the underlying insight without necessarily adopting the exact solution as-is.
4. Describes a real attempt to reduce how often this happens — giving that person more regular context or visibility into ongoing work.

## Warning Signs

1. Simply implements what was handed over without engaging with the underlying problem.
2. Dismisses the input outright with no attempt to understand what prompted it.
3. No account of what changed afterward — the same pattern just repeats.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Real engagement with the underlying problem, a thoughtful (not automatic) decision about what to actually build, and a real change in the pattern going forward.
- **3:** Some engagement occurs but the outcome or the follow-through is thin.
- **1:** Pure compliance or pure dismissal, with no real engagement.
