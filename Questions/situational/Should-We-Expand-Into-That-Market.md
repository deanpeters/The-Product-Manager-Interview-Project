# Should We Expand Into That Market?

> Leadership is debating whether to expand into an adjacent market or customer segment — not a new feature, a genuinely new market. Walk me through how you'd actually evaluate whether that's the right move.

> **Follow-up** What would make you say no, even if the market looks big?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization

## Why This Matters

"Should we expand into X" is one of the most consequential, most speculative questions a product leader gets asked, and it's easy to answer with enthusiasm instead of a real evaluation. This question tests whether a candidate has an actual method for reasoning about expansion — not just a market-sizing number, but organizational fit, distribution, and what would have to be true for it to work — and is willing to conclude the answer might be no.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would you actually need to know before forming a view — be specific about the first three questions?
- How would you tell the difference between a market that's genuinely reachable versus one that just looks big on paper?
- What existing strengths or distribution would you actually be able to reuse, versus starting from zero?

## Strong Evidence

1. Goes beyond market size to ask about reachability, distribution, and organizational fit — not just "is the market big."
2. Names specific evidence they'd want before forming a view, not just intuition.
3. Distinguishes what capabilities or distribution the company could actually reuse from what would need to be built from scratch.
4. Gives a real, specific condition under which they'd say no — not a hedge that avoids ever taking a position.

## Warning Signs

1. Answer is enthusiasm without evaluation — "sounds exciting" with no real method.
2. Relies only on market size (TAM) with no reachability or fit analysis.
3. Can't articulate any condition that would make them say no — every market sounds worth entering.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Real evaluation method covering reachability and organizational fit, specific evidence named, and a genuine no-condition articulated.
- **3:** Some real reasoning but leans heavily on market size alone, or the no-condition is vague.
- **1:** Pure enthusiasm with no real evaluation method.
