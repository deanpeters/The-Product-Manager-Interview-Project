# A Decision You Got Wrong

> Tell me about a product decision you got wrong. Not something the team got wrong — something you got wrong.

> **Follow-up** What's an important Product Management belief you held a few years ago that you no longer believe? What changed your mind?

## Type

Behavioral

## Competencies Assessed

- Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making

## Why This Matters

Candidates rehearse success stories; genuine self-awareness shows up in how honestly and specifically they describe their own mistakes. This question filters for real accountability — a decision they personally made and would make differently now — rather than a diplomatically reframed team failure.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What did you believe at the time that turned out to be wrong?
- What was the actual consequence of the mistake?
- What would you do differently if you faced the same situation today?
- What changed your mind on the belief in the follow-up — a specific event, or a gradual shift?

## Strong Evidence

1. Owns a specific decision as their own, not a team's or a stakeholder's, with a real named consequence.
2. Explains what they believed at the time and why it turned out to be wrong, showing the reasoning error rather than just the bad outcome.
3. Describes what they'd do differently now, showing the mistake actually changed their practice.
4. The belief-change follow-up names something substantive, not a safe or trivial example.

## Warning Signs

1. "Mistake" is really a team or company failure with the candidate at a safe distance.
2. No real consequence described — a low-stakes or inconsequential example.
3. Belief-change answer is generic ("I used to move faster, now I'm more careful") without specifics.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific, personally-owned mistake with a real consequence, clear reasoning about what was wrong, and a substantive belief change in the follow-up.
- **3:** A real mistake is named but ownership or the "what changed" reasoning is somewhat soft.
- **1:** Deflects ownership to the team, or offers no real example.
