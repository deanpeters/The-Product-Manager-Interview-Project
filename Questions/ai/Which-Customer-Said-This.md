# Which Customer Said This?

> An AI tool synthesized your team's last round of customer interviews into a clean, confident theme — something like "customers want more automation in the approval flow." It's a compelling story and everyone in the room wants to believe it. How would you verify it's real signal before it lands on the roadmap?

> **Follow-up** Suppose you try to trace the theme back to source quotes and can't cleanly find them — the interviews say something close, but not quite that. What do you do next?

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). AI-generated research synthesis is fast, fluent, and confident-sounding — which makes it easy to mistake pattern-matching for genuine customer signal. A model summarizing interview transcripts can smooth over contradictions, blend distinct customers' language into one composite voice, or surface a "theme" that's really a coincidental resemblance across a few responses. This card tests whether a candidate treats an AI-synthesized insight as a hypothesis to verify, not a conclusion to act on — the specific discipline of tracing a claim back to a real source quote before it becomes a roadmap decision.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would you actually check to confirm the theme is real, beyond re-reading the summary?
- How would you tell the difference between three customers saying the same thing and one AI-generated composite that sounds like three customers agreeing?
- Who else would you want to weigh in before treating this as a confirmed pattern?
- How has this changed how you use AI synthesis tools in discovery work, if at all?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a concrete verification step — tracing the theme back to specific transcripts, quotes, or timestamps — rather than accepting the synthesis at face value.
2. Articulates a real way to distinguish genuine convergence across customers from an AI-smoothed composite that reads as convergence.
3. In the follow-up, has a real next step when the trace doesn't cleanly hold up — narrowing the claim, going back to the source customers, or discarding the theme — rather than forcing the story to fit.
4. Shows judgment about when AI synthesis is fast and trustworthy enough to lean on versus when it needs manual verification, not a blanket policy in either direction.

## Warning Signs

1. Treats the AI-generated synthesis as ground truth with no verification step at all.
2. Can't articulate any concrete difference between real convergence and AI-smoothed convergence.
3. In the follow-up, forces the theme to still be true rather than genuinely reconsidering it once the trace fails.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Concrete, specific verification method; a real articulated distinction between genuine and fabricated convergence; and an honest, evidence-driven next step when the trace doesn't hold up.
- **3:** Some verification instinct present, but vague about method or inconsistent about what would actually change their mind.
- **1:** Accepts the synthesis uncritically, or has no real method for telling real signal from fabricated pattern.
