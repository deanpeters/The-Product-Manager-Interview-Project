# Interviewer Voices

## Purpose

Four named interviewer personas, each built on the pressure levels in [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](evidence-and-pressure-standard.md), so a round can have a consistent, memorable style rather than each interviewer improvising their own tone. A voice changes *how* a round is run — its emphasis and follow-up style — not *which* card is asked or what counts as a good answer; the card's own Strong Evidence and Warning Signs sections are still the actual scoring basis.

**This is optional.** Most rounds work fine run in a plain, neutral style. Reach for a named voice when a round would benefit from a consistent emphasis — especially useful for Director/VP loops, or when multiple interviewers are running the same round across candidates and want a shared, repeatable style rather than each defaulting to their own instincts.

**Read the Candidate Safety Cap in [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](evidence-and-pressure-standard.md) before using any of these.** Every voice below defaults to `constructive` or `skeptical`; the two that can run at `resistant` are marked, and none of them should ever run at `hostile-room` against a real candidate.

## The Voices

### The Curious Peer

**Default pressure:** `constructive`

**Emphasis:** Genuine exploration of how the candidate thinks, not testing them against a hidden bar. Asks "what did you consider that you didn't end up doing?" more than "how do you know that's true?"

**Sample follow-up phrasing:** "That's interesting — walk me through what else you looked at before landing there." / "What would you want to know if you were doing this again?"

**Best fit:** Round 1 (Customer Understanding And Discovery), early-career or first-round candidates, or any round where the goal is understanding how someone thinks rather than testing them under scrutiny.

### The Skeptical Engineer

**Default pressure:** `skeptical`, can run at `resistant` for senior technical/platform roles

**Emphasis:** Feasibility and causal claims. Doesn't accept "it worked" without asking how the candidate knows, and specifically probes the mechanism behind a claimed result.

**Sample follow-up phrasing:** "How do you actually know that was the cause, and not something else that changed at the same time?" / "Walk me through what would have had to be true for that to work."

**Best fit:** Round 2 (Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization), Round 3's technical sizing check, and the AI-competency Round 5 cards — anywhere a causal or feasibility claim is the thing being evaluated.

### The Time-Pressed Executive

**Default pressure:** `skeptical`, can run at `resistant` for Director/VP rounds

**Emphasis:** Bottom line first, then evidence. Tests whether a candidate can prioritize what matters in their own answer, not just whether they have a good story somewhere in there.

**Sample follow-up phrasing:** "Give me the one-sentence version first — then we'll go deeper." / "If you only had two minutes to convince me, what would you lead with?"

**Best fit:** Round 2's decision-defense cards ([The Business Case Behind the Ask](../Questions/judgment/The-Business-Case-Behind-The-Ask.md), [Defending a Roadmap Trade-off](../Questions/judgment/Defending-a-Roadmap-Tradeoff.md)) and Round 3's [Prepping for a Room You Don't Control](../Questions/behavioral/Prepping-for-a-Room-You-Dont-Control.md) — this voice is a natural fit for a card that's already about handling exactly this kind of pressure.

### The Accountability Partner

**Default pressure:** `skeptical` — never runs at `resistant`; this voice's job is depth, not resistance

**Emphasis:** Won't let a candidate deflect ownership onto "the team" or circumstances. Keeps asking "and then what did you do" until it's clear whose decision it actually was.

**Sample follow-up phrasing:** "That's what the team did — what did *you* specifically do?" / "What would you do differently if this came up again tomorrow?"

**Best fit:** Round 4 (Ethics, Self-Awareness, And Responsible Decision-Making) — pairs naturally with [A Decision You Got Wrong](../Questions/behavioral/A-Decision-You-Got-Wrong.md) and [When Growth Could Harm Customers](../Questions/judgment/When-Growth-Could-Harm-Customers.md).

## Using A Voice

1. **Pick a voice per round, not per question.** Switching voices mid-round to chase a specific answer looks like — and functions like — moving the goalposts.
2. **Note the voice used on the [scorecard](interview-scorecard-template.md)** so the record shows what tone the candidate actually experienced, which matters if scores are later compared across interviewers who used different voices.
3. **A voice is still the interviewer, not a character.** Unlike a rehearsal exercise where a colleague plays a stakeholder persona, an interviewer using one of these voices is still recognizably themselves conducting a real interview — the voice shapes emphasis and follow-up style, not identity.
4. **Every candidate for the same role should get the same voice for the same round**, for the same reason every candidate gets the same questions — comparability. Don't run The Curious Peer for a candidate you already favor and The Skeptical Engineer for one you don't.

## What This Isn't

This isn't a script to perform or a character to roleplay at the candidate — see the Candidate Safety Cap in [evidence-and-pressure-standard.md](evidence-and-pressure-standard.md). It's a consistent lens for follow-up style, meant to make interviews more comparable across interviewers, not more theatrical.
