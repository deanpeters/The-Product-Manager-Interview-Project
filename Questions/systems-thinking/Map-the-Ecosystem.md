# Map the Ecosystem

> Pick a product, workflow, or market you know well. Map the actors, incentives, constraints, and feedback loops that determine whether it succeeds.

> **Follow-up** Where would the system push back if you tried to change it?

## Type

Systems Thinking Exercise

## Competencies Assessed

- Product Judgment, Strategy, And Prioritization
- Customer Understanding And Discovery
- Influence, Collaboration, And Communication

## Why This Matters

Products do not succeed in isolation. This exercise tests whether a candidate can see the surrounding system: who participates, what each actor wants, what constraints shape behavior, and which loops reinforce or resist change.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- Who are the actors in the system besides the buyer and the end user?
- What does each actor want, and where do those incentives conflict?
- What flows through the system: money, work, information, risk, trust, inventory, time?
- What feedback loop would tell you the system is getting healthier or sicker?
- If you could only observe three signals for a month, which would you choose?

## Strong Evidence

1. Maps multiple actor groups and distinguishes their different incentives rather than collapsing everyone into "the user."
2. Names constraints that shape behavior: operational limits, policy, switching costs, trust, data, channel dynamics, or internal team capacity.
3. Identifies at least one feedback loop that could reinforce success or create a failure pattern.
4. Connects the system map to a decision: where to intervene, what to measure, or what risk to reduce first.
5. Notices second-order effects rather than treating the product change as isolated from the surrounding workflow.

## Warning Signs

1. Describes only product features or screens, with no actor map or incentive model.
2. Treats all participants as wanting the same thing.
3. Lists many parts of the system but cannot explain how they interact.
4. Has no feedback-loop or measurement logic for knowing whether the system is improving.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Clear actor map, specific incentives and constraints, at least one real feedback loop, and a plausible intervention or measurement choice tied to the system.
- **3:** Identifies several actors and constraints, but the relationships, feedback loops, or decision implications are thin.
- **1:** Stays at the feature-list level, or produces a generic stakeholder list with no system dynamics.

## Role Or Level Notes

For senior candidates, expect them to include company operating constraints, cross-functional incentives, and second-order consequences. For Director/VP candidates, expect a clear view of where the system could be changed through strategy, operating model, pricing, policy, or organizational design, not only product features.
