# Designing for a Constraint You Don't Share

> How would you design an alarm clock for someone who is blind?

> **Follow-up** Before you describe a design, what would you want to know first?

## Type

Situational

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery

## Why This Matters

This question isn't really about alarm clocks — it's about whether a candidate reaches for questions before reaching for a solution when the user's context is different from their own. A PM who designs confidently for a constraint they don't personally share, without first asking who the user is, is a warning sign regardless of how clever the eventual design is.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What would you want to know about the range of users — fully blind, partially sighted, other needs?
- Would you look at how this person already solves this problem today, before designing something new?
- Who else — a sighted partner, a caregiver — might also interact with this device, and does that change anything?

## Strong Evidence

1. Leads with discovery questions rather than jumping straight to a design, and keeps asking rather than stopping after one or two.
2. Asks about the range within "blind" (fully blind vs. low vision) rather than treating it as one undifferentiated group.
3. Asks whether the user already has a mental model from an existing product (a regular alarm clock) worth building on.
4. When they do describe design ideas, they connect each one back to a specific need surfaced by their own questions — e.g., voice control, large tactile or braille controls, a weighted base, a visual display for a sighted partner.

## Warning Signs

1. Jumps directly into a design with fewer than two or three clarifying questions.
2. Treats "blind users" as a single homogeneous group with one obvious set of needs.
3. Never asks about context of use (where the clock lives, how it's powered, who else uses it).
4. Design ideas feel generic or novelty-driven rather than tied back to a stated user need.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Substantial, well-targeted discovery questions before any design, explicit differentiation within the user group, and design ideas that clearly trace back to specific answers.
- **3:** Asks a few relevant questions but moves to design earlier than ideal; design ideas are reasonable but not clearly evidence-driven.
- **1:** Designs immediately with little or no clarifying discovery, or treats the user group as uniform.

## Role Or Level Notes

Useful as an early-stage discovery check for any PM role; not intended as a deep accessibility-expertise assessment on its own.
