# Systems Thinking Exercises

## Purpose

`Questions/systems-thinking/` is for exercises that ask a candidate or learner to break down an unfamiliar situation as a system: actors, incentives, constraints, flows, feedback loops, dependencies, unknowns, and useful precedents.

These exercises are not part of the 58-card active interview bank yet. They become active only after an explicit promotion decision updates the core loop and card count.

## Current Candidates

- [Map the Ecosystem](../Questions/systems-thinking/Map-the-Ecosystem.md) — foundational system decomposition: actors, incentives, constraints, flows, and feedback loops.
- [The Precedent That Transfers](../Questions/systems-thinking/The-Precedent-That-Transfers.md) — Precedents Thinking: analogous systems, transfer limits, recombination, and testability.
- [One Fix, Three Side Effects](../Questions/systems-thinking/One-Fix-Three-Side-Effects.md) — second-order effects, local optimization, guardrails, and system-level harm.
- [Map the Vending Machine System](../Questions/systems-thinking/ProductDesign-VendingMachine.md) — ecosystem mapping and intervention-point reasoning, reframed from an older product-design brainstorm.
- [Model the Facility Before You Count](../Questions/systems-thinking/Number-of-Pianos.md) — estimation-as-modeling for an operational decision, reframed from an older counting prompt.

## What Belongs Here

Good candidates for this folder include:

- Problem decomposition exercises where the point is how someone frames and breaks down the system before proposing a solution.
- Precedents Thinking exercises where someone finds analogous systems, extracts useful patterns, and recombines them into a testable approach.
- Estimation-as-modeling exercises where the useful signal is the structure of the model, not the exact number.
- Product ecology exercises that look at stakeholders, incentives, supply, demand, operations, economics, data, regulation, and feedback loops together.
- Teaching examples that show why an older brainstorm or puzzle needs a stronger rubric before it can be used in hiring.

## What Does Not Belong Here

Do not use this folder as a dumping ground for questions that merely feel old, clever, or hard to classify.

Avoid adding:

- Brainteasers whose only signal is whether someone has seen the trick before.
- Abstract design prompts that reward idea volume without evaluating reasoning.
- Market-sizing puzzles where the interviewer cares about the answer more than the model.
- Framework-recitation prompts that test vocabulary instead of judgment.

## Precedents Thinking Fit

Precedents Thinking fits this lane when the exercise asks the candidate to:

1. Frame the real challenge before solving it.
2. Decompose the system into parts that can be compared.
3. Search for analogous systems or prior patterns.
4. Extract what transfers and what does not.
5. Recombine the useful pieces into a possible approach.
6. Name what would need to be tested before acting.

The hiring signal is not whether the candidate knows the phrase "Precedents Thinking." The signal is whether they can reason from structure, analogy, evidence, and limits without forcing a copied solution into a different context.

## Promotion Standard

To promote a systems-thinking exercise into the active bank, confirm or add:

- The specific competency it assesses.
- The system breakdown behavior the interviewer should listen for.
- Neutral probes that deepen reasoning without steering the answer.
- Strong evidence and warning signs that two interviewers could apply consistently.
- Scoring anchors that reward decomposition, transfer judgment, and testability.

Then update `docs/question-index.md`, `docs/question-bank-triage.md`, `docs/core-interview-loop.md`, relevant role presets, and the README card count if the active-bank total changes.
