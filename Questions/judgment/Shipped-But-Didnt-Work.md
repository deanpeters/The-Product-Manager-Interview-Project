# Shipped, But Didn't Work

> Tell me about something your product team shipped successfully that nevertheless failed to produce the outcome you wanted.

> **Follow-up** Tell me about a time you had to redirect a conversation from "Are we delivering?" to "Is this actually working?"

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Delivery, Execution, And Ownership
- Metrics, Outcomes, And Learning

## Why This Matters

Shipping is not the same as succeeding. This question directly tests whether a candidate confuses output with outcome — a common and costly failure mode — and whether they're willing to hold themselves accountable for a launch that technically worked but didn't move the needle.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- How did you know it wasn't working — what did you measure, and when did you know?
- What did you do once you realized it wasn't producing the outcome?
- Whose expectations did you have to reset, and how did that conversation go?

## Strong Evidence

1. Clearly separates "we shipped it" from "it worked," with a specific outcome metric or signal that fell short.
2. Describes concrete action taken after realizing the gap — iterate, pivot, or stop — rather than just noting the disappointment.
3. In the follow-up, describes a real instance of shifting a team or stakeholder's attention from delivery status to outcome evidence, including how that landed.
4. Takes visible ownership of the miss rather than attributing it entirely to external factors.

## Warning Signs

1. Treats "we shipped on time" as the success story itself, with no real outcome discussion.
2. No specific metric or evidence for how they knew it wasn't working.
3. Follow-up answer describes only project-status conversations, not outcome conversations.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific outcome gap named with evidence, concrete follow-up action, and a real example of redirecting a team from delivery to outcome focus.
- **3:** A real shortfall is described, but the response taken or the redirect example is vague.
- **1:** Conflates delivery with success throughout, or no example of holding an outcome standard.
