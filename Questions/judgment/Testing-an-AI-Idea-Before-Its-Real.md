# Testing an AI Idea Before It's Real

> Tell me about how you validated an AI-powered feature idea before committing real engineering time to build it. What made you confident — or not — that it would actually work?

> **Follow-up** What was uncertain about *feasibility* specifically, separate from whether customers would want it?

## Type

Judgment

## Competencies Assessed

- Customer Understanding And Discovery

## AI Competency

[AI Product Discovery & Validation](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md#ai-product-discovery--validation)

## Why This Matters

Only use this card for roles where AI is a real part of the product surface — see [ai-competency-model.md](../../docs/ai-competency-model.md). AI features carry a distinct kind of feasibility risk beyond typical product feasibility — a model might simply not be capable of the task reliably, independent of engineering effort. This question tests whether a candidate treats an AI feature's actual behavior as something to discover cheaply, rather than assuming it away.

## Neutral Follow-Up Probes

- What did you actually build or test before the real thing — a prompt, a small prototype, a manual "Wizard of Oz" version?
- What result would have stopped you from moving forward?
- Did the validation surface something you didn't expect?

## Strong Evidence

1. Describes a real, specific validation step — not a full build — used to reduce uncertainty before committing.
2. Explicitly separates feasibility uncertainty (can the model actually do this reliably) from desirability uncertainty (do customers want it), rather than conflating them.
3. Names a real result that would have stopped the project, showing the validation had actual teeth.
4. Describes something the validation revealed that wasn't expected going in.

## Warning Signs

1. "Validation" was really just building the full feature and seeing what happened.
2. No distinction between feasibility and desirability uncertainty.
3. No real answer for what would have killed the idea — validation was a formality.

## Scoring Anchors

- **5:** Specific lightweight validation method, clear feasibility/desirability separation, a real stop condition, and an unexpected finding.
- **3:** Some validation occurred but is thin, or feasibility and desirability aren't clearly separated.
- **1:** No real validation before the build, or no way the idea could have been stopped.
